The winning model (3SSE: Spectral + band features → TabPFN (foundation model)) catches 83% of positive cases (sensitivity) and correctly clears 83% of negative cases (specificity) under patient-grouped cross-validation.

Margin mode (paired normal reference): every spectrum is classified by its deviation from the same patient's normal tissue — the same principle as intraoperative margin assessment with fiber-optic Raman (~91% accuracy in 2025 in-vivo work). New predictions need a normal reference from the same patient.

What a positive call is worth depends on where you stand: at a lesion-clinic prevalence (~30%) a positive is right about 68% of the time (PPV); in low-prevalence screening (~5%) only 21%. Negative calls stay reliable in both (92% NPV at 30%).

Calibration (Brier score): 0.143 — 0 is perfect, 0.25 is chance; the closer the probabilities are to reality, the more you can trust a 0.8 vs a 0.55.

These numbers come from patient-grouped cross-validation on 68 patients; expect larger variation on new patients. This is research triage support for trained clinicians — not a medical diagnosis; histopathology remains the reference standard.
